# Principles

oh-my-usage is read at a glance, many times a day, by someone deciding where the next piece of work can go. Every
screen is judged by how fast it answers, in this order:

1. **How much is used?** Each account's main window, the one it lives on: one big figure, drawn over its week.
2. **Can it take work right now?** Active, paused or blocked, and its short window (Claude's 5-hour session).
3. **Is trouble here?** Only when it is: a window near its limit `▲`, one spent `■`, a saved reset about to lapse. No
   news is no news.

Anything else (other windows, price, saved resets, burn rate) is there for a second look, not the first.

## Rules for a glance

**The biggest thing is the most important thing.** One display-size figure per account: the used share of its main
window. Nothing else on the row competes with it.

**The history is the row.** An account's week is drawn the full height of its row in its provider's colour, ending in
a dot at now. On a wide screen it fades in where the words end and runs to the row's end, so nothing written sits on
it and its start lines up from row to row. On a narrow one it runs behind the whole row, top to foot and end to end, with the words over it.
A chart in a small box is a chart nobody reads.

**One account, one row.** At every width, each account is a single line of the page, and figures sit in the same place
from row to row so a column can be scanned. On a narrow screen a row's parts stack; rows never become cards in a
grid.

**In the order the gateway picks.** The list follows the gateway's own order for a new conversation, provider by
provider, mirrored from omp's ranking: what would go to waste soonest first, a saved reset about to lapse counting as
its deadline; a nearly full short window or the reserve later; blocked, spent or paused last. Each row says its place
and why in a few quiet words beside the name: `1st · reset lapses in 2d`, `later · 5h nearly full`, `last`.

**Dense, not cramped.** Every pixel shows data or the gap that separates it. No empty columns, no labels that repeat
what the layout already says, no decoration that carries nothing. Fill the width with the chart, not with space.

**Secondary stays small and quiet.** Price, resets and burn rate are small and muted, on solid ground beside the
name. The other windows live in the row's chart, under the main one and on its scale: one that lasts about as long
(a model's week) is a thin grey line, dashed when there are two; one far shorter (a five-hour session) would only
saw up and down, so it stands as quiet bars, one a stretch of its own length, as high as it got, behind the glow, so
a session that hit its limit stands as tall as the row. Each is named on a small chip short of the row's end, at its
own level and moved apart from the others: a short tag (`5h`, `Fable`), its share, marked when in trouble, and when
it resets if that is not when the main window does. On a narrow screen the chips give way to a line of the same
words under the row.

**Controls wait to be wanted.** With a broker, a small round button over the end of the chart pauses an account. With
a mouse it shows only when the row is pointed at (or reached with the keyboard), so the chart runs clear; on a touch
screen it is always there. A paused account keeps its button lit, a triangle to resume. It turns at once, and back if
the broker refuses. Taking work is the normal state and says nothing; paused or blocked is a word beside the name.

**Alarm only on trouble.** Colour and marks (`▲` near the limit, `■` spent) appear only when something needs a person.
No forecasts: where usage is heading is the pace beside the reset, not an alarm. A calm fleet looks calm.

**A saved reset is spent or lost.** The resets an account holds are a small chip first on the line under its name: how
many can be spent of how many it holds, and when the first that can be spent expires (`↻ 1/3 · 20d`), the whole
sentence on hover. One that expires before the window it clears resets on its own has no later window to be spent in:
that is trouble, so the chip turns to the warning colour, `▲ spend 1 in 2d`. Until then it is quiet, since spending a reset early gives back only what the window has used.

**Say how old it is.** Data that is old says so; a value is never shown as current when it is not.

**Never colour alone.** Every state is a word or a mark as well as a colour; see [Introduction](?path=/docs/foundations-introduction--docs).

## The band at the top

The page opens on one band, headed by the product's mark and name, which lead home, with a hairline in the mark's
gradient along its top. Under the name it answers the first question for every provider at once: the share of its main
window used across all its accounts, as a figure and a meter in its colour, and when the next account resets. One line
each, so the figures fall in columns.

Beside it, with a broker, the gateway's traffic as it happens, drawn edge to edge over the last ten minutes with each
provider stacked in its colour, and what the hour, day and week came to at API prices on a single chip. It opens on
tokens out a second; a switch, each choice carrying its own figure now, redraws it for tokens in, the share read from a
cache (one line, from the tenth below its lowest point, which is named), the cost an hour or requests a minute. Its
heading and its chart lead to the Traffic page, which opens on the same choice. It is the only part of the page that
moves: it glides left as each reading comes in, its figure rolls, and its newest point pings while the readings are
fresh. Stale, it says so in a word; with reduced motion it holds still.

There is no bar of pages and no line of fleet totals: the rows say it. Elsewhere a page opens on the mark, which leads
home, beside where the page sits. A stream that is down is named beside the mark, wherever it is.

## Before you change a page

Ask what the change answers, and where it sits in the three questions. If it answers none of them, it belongs on the
account's own page, not the list. If it competes with the big figure, it is too loud.
